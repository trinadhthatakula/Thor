Shizuku mode removes it for your Android user instead, with the keep-data flag — the app's files and settings stay, so unfreezing brings it back with its data attached. Permissions are the part I can only tell you about narrowly: one runtime permission was measured across that round trip and came back granted, on one platform build, and app-ops were never measured, so I will not promise more than that.

The measurement was of a single permission granted from the shell rather than by tapping Allow. A permission you granted yourself in Settings was not measured, and neither was any app-op, so read the result for the scope it has.

What the keep-data flag does establish is about files: the data directories keep their inodes across the round trip, which is why unfreezing reattaches the app to the state you left it in rather than to an empty one.
