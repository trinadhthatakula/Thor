Earlier builds had a second path for an app the platform refused to switch off, and the permission evidence here was taken across it. That is the part I can only tell you about narrowly: one runtime permission was measured across that round trip and came back granted, on one platform build, and app-ops were never measured, so I will not promise more than that.

The measurement was of a single permission granted from the shell rather than by tapping Allow. A permission you granted yourself in Settings was not measured, and neither was any app-op, so read the result for the scope it has.

What the keep-data flag did establish was about files: the data directories kept their inodes across the round trip, which is why unfreezing reattached the app to the state you left it in rather than to an empty one. None of that is load-bearing now — your app data stays where it is because a freeze switches a package off and takes nothing away.
